What's real on Windows and what's approximated
  • Real: the frosted blur of whatever is behind the dock. It uses Windows' own acrylic material, the same effect the Windows 11 Start menu uses.
  • Real: the water, drops, rim light and wobble. We draw these in Qt Quick on the GPU, and they only animate during a change (about 0.5 s). The rest of the time nothing redraws, so there's no battery cost.
  • Approximated: Apple's true refraction, where content visibly bends at the glass edge. Windows can't do that without screen-capturing behind the dock 30 times a second. The rim highlights you see here are the stand-in.
  • Deliberately calm: nothing moves when nothing changes. The dock sits in your peripheral vision under the camera, and motion there would pull your eyes back to the laptop and cause false switches.
  • Always full glass and motion: the dock ignores Windows' "Animation effects" and "Transparency effects" settings, as you asked.
  • Shape cues as well as colour: a bigger tile is where focus is, a lid means frozen while you type, an empty dock means no face, and bars mean paused.
